# **1\. Document Purpose**

This document consolidates the approved website strategy, sitemap, page architecture, public-facing copy, visual direction and implementation requirements for the redesign of H2MB’s corporate website. It is intended to give the web developer a single source of truth from which to create wireframes, visual designs and the final website.

| Core positioning test: The finished website must look like a company developing energy infrastructure, not simply a company explaining hydrogen. |
| :---- |

## **Primary website objective**

Establish H2MB as a credible emerging Canadian energy infrastructure company developing Phase 1 of a broader green hydrogen production and distribution strategy in Manitoba.

## **Primary audiences**

* Potential strategic and private investors  
* Government and economic-development stakeholders  
* Prospective hydrogen customers and offtake partners  
* Transportation, industrial, power and agricultural organizations  
* Technology, engineering and infrastructure partners  
* Media, community stakeholders and the general public

## **Public-disclosure principle**

The public website should explain what H2MB is developing, why it matters and how organizations can participate. It should not disclose sensitive engineering, commercial or financing information.

| Appropriate for the public website | Keep in controlled materials |
| :---- | :---- |
| High-level Phase 1 purpose and integrated capabilities | Detailed site plans, engineering drawings or exact equipment configuration |
| Green hydrogen production, storage, distribution and refuelling | Supplier pricing, procurement terms or non-public vendor selections |
| Development-stage language and broad progress | Detailed CAPEX, financing structure, valuation or project returns |
| Broad market applications and partnership categories | Names or details of confidential customer and offtake discussions |
| General future-growth strategy | Unapproved future-phase capacities, dates or commitments |

# **3\. Brand, Visual & Interaction Direction**

## **Desired character**

Established energy-company credibility \+ modern clean-energy optimism \+ a distinctly Manitoba identity.

| Influence | Approximate weight | Meaning |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Major energy / infrastructure company | 70% | Substantial, disciplined, credible and project-led. |
| Modern clean-energy developer | 20% | Optimistic, spacious and forward-looking. |
| Technology / innovation | 10% | Clear systems thinking, restrained motion and technical linework. |

## **Reference direction**

* EverWind: overall storytelling confidence, regional identity, generous whitespace and project-first narrative.  
* Air Products and Linde: industrial discipline and the sense that energy infrastructure is substantial.  
* Plug Power: simple visual explanation of the hydrogen value chain.  
* These are directional references only. H2MB must not reproduce their imagery, facility designs, trade dress or specific components.

## **Colour system**

| Role | Direction |
| :---- | :---- |
| Primary | Deep navy / near black for credibility and major section backgrounds. |
| Accent | Existing H2MB green, used selectively for buttons, rules, active states and diagrams. |
| Light backgrounds | Warm white and cool light grey. |
| Text | Near-black on light backgrounds; white on dark or photographic backgrounds. |

Avoid making the website predominantly green. Alternating white, photographic, navy and light-grey sections will make the green accent more effective. The website needs to be clean. 

## **Typography and layout**

* Use a modern grotesk or architectural sans-serif with a broad family of weights.  
* Major headings should be large, confident and selectively uppercase. Body copy remains sentence case.  
* Use wide layouts, full-width imagery and a desktop content container of approximately 1,300–1,450 pixels.  
* Provide generous vertical spacing. Allow one major idea to dominate each viewport where appropriate.  
* Use square or very slightly rounded corners. Avoid a software/SaaS aesthetic with oversized rounded cards.

## **Motion**

* Use restrained scroll reveals, subtle image movement and line-drawing animations.  
* Use animation to explain systems, especially renewable power → production → storage → distribution → customer.  
* Respect reduced-motion accessibility settings.  
* Avoid theatrical page transitions, floating elements, constant motion or futuristic interface effects.

# **4\. Imagery Policy**

| Critical instruction: Do not use images of another company’s hydrogen facility, proprietary equipment, branded vehicles or site and present them in a way that could be interpreted as H2MB’s assets or project. |
| :---- |

## **Preferred imagery**

* General Manitoba landscapes, prairie horizons, Winnipeg context and transportation corridors.  
* Unbranded heavy transportation and freight imagery that illustrates potential applications at a category level.  
* General renewable electricity, hydroelectric landscape and grid imagery that does not imply ownership.  
* Abstract or close-cropped industrial textures, piping, power lines and infrastructure details with no visible third-party branding.  
* Custom H2MB conceptual renderings, clearly labelled “Conceptual rendering” wherever needed.  
* Custom diagrams, maps and line illustrations created specifically for H2MB.  
* Authentic H2MB team, stakeholder, event and project-development photography when available and approved.

## **Avoid**

* Recognizable third-party hydrogen plants, electrolyzers, storage systems or refuelling stations.  
* Images containing other company names, logos or distinctive brand colours.  
* Technical equipment that is not representative of H2MB’s intended project.  
* Generic greenwashing clichés such as hands holding seedlings, glowing molecules or excessive wind-turbine imagery.  
* AI-generated technical imagery that may imply engineering accuracy.  
* Photorealistic imagery that blurs the distinction between a conceptual project and a completed facility.

## **Required captions and alt text**

Conceptual facility imagery must use a visible or adjacent “Conceptual rendering” label. Alt text should describe the image plainly and must not claim a facility is operating. For example: “Conceptual rendering of a future H2MB green hydrogen facility in Manitoba.”

## **Section 2 — Green Hydrogen**

**GREEN HYDROGEN**

**Not all hydrogen is produced the same way.**

Hydrogen is an energy carrier that can be produced through different processes. Green hydrogen is made by using renewable electricity to separate water into hydrogen and oxygen through electrolysis. When produced this way, it can offer a low-carbon energy pathway for suitable applications.

| Traditional hydrogen production | Green hydrogen production |
| :---- | :---- |
| Often relies on fossil fuels and may create significant greenhouse gas emissions. | Uses renewable electricity and water through electrolysis, avoiding fossil-fuel combustion in the production process. |

## **Section 3 — How It Works**

**HOW IT WORKS**

**From renewable electricity to energy where it is needed.**

Green hydrogen creates a way to convert renewable electricity into an energy carrier that can be stored, transported and used later.

| 01 | 02 | 03 | 04 | 05 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Renewable electricity | Water is separated through electrolysis | Green hydrogen is produced | Hydrogen is stored and distributed | Energy is delivered to suitable end uses |

## **Section 4 — Why Manitoba**

## **Section 5 — Applications**

**HYDROGEN APPLICATIONS**

**Where hydrogen could provide practical value.**

Hydrogen is not the answer for every energy use. Its strongest potential is in applications where direct electrification is difficult, operational requirements are demanding or hydrogen is needed as a feedstock.

### **Heavy Transportation**

Hydrogen may support certain heavy-duty trucking, fleet and commercial transportation applications where payload, range, utilization and refuelling time are important. H2MB is working to understand how regional hydrogen supply and infrastructure can support the evolution of Manitoba’s transportation sector.

### **Power & Energy**

Hydrogen can potentially support power generation, distributed energy, backup or resilient energy systems and other emerging applications. The right use depends on operational requirements, economics and the availability of suitable equipment and infrastructure.

### **Industry**

Industrial users may consider hydrogen for process energy, manufacturing, chemical applications and other operations that are difficult to electrify directly. H2MB is interested in practical opportunities where regional green hydrogen could contribute to lower-carbon operations.

### **Agriculture**

Over time, hydrogen may contribute to agricultural equipment, low-carbon fuels, fertilizer-related value chains and other rural energy applications. These opportunities will develop at different rates and require collaboration across producers, technology providers and users.

### **Emerging Opportunities**

Hydrogen may also support future fuel pathways and new technologies, including select aviation, synthetic fuel and energy applications. H2MB will evaluate opportunities as technology, policy and commercial demand evolve.

**THE OPPORTUNITY**

**Building supply and demand together.**

Hydrogen markets face a connected challenge: users need confidence that supply and infrastructure will be available, while producers need confidence that demand will develop. H2MB’s role is to help connect these parts of the market and support responsible growth over time.

## **CMS-managed content**

* Approved ecosystem logos and relationship labels  
* Leadership profiles, biographies and links  
* Contact form routing and dropdown options  
* SEO titles, meta descriptions and social-sharing images  
* Global footer contact details and legal links

## **Core functionality**

* Responsive behaviour across desktop, tablet and mobile  
* Accessible same-page anchor navigation  
* News filtering by All, H2MB News, Insights and Media  
* Individual article templates and related-story logic  
* Contact form with spam protection, validation and success/error states  
* Analytics and conversion-event tracking for key CTAs and form submissions  
* Editable image captions, credits and alt text  
* Open Graph and social-sharing metadata  
* XML sitemap, robots configuration, canonical URLs and structured data where appropriate

## **Accessibility**

* Target WCAG 2.2 AA.  
* Maintain sufficient colour contrast, visible keyboard focus and logical tab order.  
* Use semantic heading order with one H1 per page.  
* Provide descriptive alt text; decorative images should use empty alt attributes.  
* Ensure animations can be disabled and do not block comprehension.  
* Use accessible form labels, errors and confirmations.  
* Do not embed essential text inside images.

## **Performance**

* Prioritize fast loading, particularly for the homepage hero.  
* Use responsive image formats and sizes, modern compression and lazy loading below the fold.  
* Avoid autoplay video unless performance and accessibility requirements are satisfied.  
* Keep animation libraries lightweight.  
* Prevent cumulative layout shift by reserving media dimensions.

# **13\. Page Design Pattern**

| Component | Direction |
| :---- | :---- |
| Hero | Large statement, minimal copy, one primary and optional secondary CTA. |
| Section labels | Small uppercase eyebrow in H2MB green. |
| Major headings | Large navy or white architectural typography. |
| Body copy | Short paragraphs, approximately 55–75 characters per line. |
| Cards | Square or subtly rounded; restrained borders; no decorative excess. |
| Diagrams | Original linework using navy, green, grey and white. |
| Dark sections | Use for vision, partner CTAs and major transitions. |
| Image overlays | Maintain high text contrast; avoid putting long copy over busy photography. |
| Section navigator | Compact anchor navigation with clear active state. |
| Mobile | Preserve the narrative order; diagrams may stack vertically. |

## **Responsive content priorities**

* Do not shrink desktop diagrams until labels become unreadable; redesign them as vertical mobile sequences.  
* Keep primary CTAs visible without crowding the mobile hero.  
* Collapse four- and five-column card rows into a logical one-column sequence.  
* Keep page-level navigation horizontally scrollable or use an accessible compact menu.  
* Crop imagery deliberately so Manitoba, transportation or infrastructure context remains visible.

# **16\. Final Positioning Summary**

The new H2MB website should lead with the company, the proposed Phase 1 infrastructure and Manitoba’s strategic opportunity. Hydrogen education supports that story, but it should not dominate it. The visitor journey should move from credibility to tangible development, then to the wider opportunity and a clear invitation to engage.

**The intended reaction is:**

* “This looks like an emerging Canadian energy infrastructure company.”  
* “H2MB is developing something tangible, while communicating its stage responsibly.”  
* “Manitoba provides a credible foundation for green hydrogen.”  
* “Phase 1 is the beginning of a broader long-term strategy.”  
* “There is a clear way for our organization to start a conversation.”

**H2MB → MANITOBA → INFRASTRUCTURE → PRODUCTION → MARKETS → PARTNERSHIPS → HYDROGEN EDUCATION**
